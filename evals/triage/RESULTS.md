# Triage evaluation results

Scored once on the held-out set under [RULES.md](RULES.md), whose SHA-256 was published in [a public gist](https://gist.github.com/leejpsd/b55b5aa7b3d4dccf9d0649bbc46010ba) before the first run. Model `gpt-6-luna`, through the product's own prompt and code.

## The answer

The model beats the plain rule, under the test fixed in advance.

| | Balanced accuracy of "high" |
|---|---|
| The model | **72.2%** |
| Plain rule: account younger than 30 days, or at most 1 earlier merges | 64.8% |
| Model minus rule, 95% interval from 1,000 paired bootstrap samples | 4.2 to 10.4 points |

It also misses a lot. It put 51.7% of maintainer-labelled low-effort pull requests in the high band, so nearly half got a medium or low hold. It kept 92.6% of merged first-timers out of the high band. Every miss is listed below.

## Limits

A label is one maintainer's view. Merged does not mean good. Titles and descriptions may have been edited after opening. A label like `spam` may be applied for reasons that are not about effort. This says nothing about pull requests with no label at all. All the sets come from repositories with 1,000 or more stars.

## Added after the rules were fixed

These readings were not in RULES.md, so treat them as exploration, not as tested claims.

- **Who the rule would overcharge.** The plain rule put 127 of 419 merged first-timers in the high band, and the model 31. Most first-timers have young accounts or few merges, so a rule built on those charges them the most.
- **A harder baseline, and the model does not clearly beat it.** A logistic regression on nine facts that need no reading of the text (account age, earlier merges, title and description length, lines and files changed, a linked issue, the share of documentation files, a test file touched), fitted on the 310 tuning pull requests ([`baseline.mjs`](baseline.mjs)), scored 70.4% against the model's 72.2%. The 95% interval for the difference is -1.2 to 4.8 points, which includes zero. That is with its threshold chosen on the tuning set, as the plain rule's pair was. Our first run used a 0.5 threshold and scored 66.0%. The two differ at the strict end: this baseline put 96 of 419 merged first-timers in its high group, where the model put 31, and held to the model's 31 it caught 341 of 752 low-effort pull requests, where the model caught 389. No metadata writes the reviewer's brief or quotes text aimed at AI reviewers.
- **What a hold would be,** at the demo repository's $2, $5 and $25: $14.59 on average for low-effort pull requests, and $4.25 for merged first-timers.
- **By label:** `spam` 283 of 453 high, `ai slop` 55 of 141 high, `slop` 15 of 51 high, `ai-slop` 36 of 107 high.
- **Text aimed at AI reviewers,** flagged with no injection added: 34 low-effort, 20 control, 1 unclear. We have not checked these by hand.
- **Choosing the model.** On the tuning set, `gpt-6.1-sol` scored 69.3% and `gpt-6-luna` 71.7%, against 65.1% for the plain rule. The product switched to `gpt-6-luna` before the held-out run.
- **Timing.** The held-out batch took about 12 hours to start returning results, though the Batch API allows up to a day.
